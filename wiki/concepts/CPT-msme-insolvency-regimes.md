# MSME insolvency regimes

_Status: candidate; family: other._

## Definition

Insolvency and restructuring rules for micro, small and medium enterprises, including simplified regimes adopted in some countries and their absence in others (motivating claims: CLM-0011-005, CLM-0011-006, CLM-0011-007).

Aliases: simplified insolvency procedures.

## Claims about the concept

### Descriptive

**GB, US, IT, IE, AU**

- Ribary et al. (2023) state that Because traditional insolvency procedures can be overly expensive, complex, long and ultimately ineffective for micro, small and medium enterprises, some countries (the United States, Italy, Ireland and Australia) have introduced simplified insolvency regimes for MSMEs, whereas the United Kingdom is one of the few common law countries not to have introduced MSME-specific rules, apart from those on discharge of debt for individuals and individual entrepreneurs. [CLM-0011-005]. — jurisdiction: GB, US, IT, IE, AU (comparative); basis: literature; positive form: split

### Interpretive

**GB**

- Ribary et al. (2023) read MSME insolvency regimes as follows: The UK insolvency system is not hopelessly ill-equipped to deal efficiently and effectively with distressed MSMEs, because English law offers a sufficiently flexible and modular approach to corporate restructuring; downsides nonetheless remain, as shown by company insolvencies in Q2 2023 reaching their highest level since Q2 2009. [CLM-0011-007]. — jurisdiction: GB; basis: literature

### Normative

**GB**

- Ribary et al. (2023) argue that The United Kingdom's choice not to introduce MSME-specific insolvency rules may prove to be an unfortunate policy choice, because opportunities to rescue distressed yet viable businesses may be lost and because the approach sits at odds with main international trends and recommendations, such as the World Bank's 2017 Report on the Treatment of MSME Insolvency and the European Union's proposal for a directive on Harmonising Certain Aspects of Insolvency Law. [CLM-0011-006]. — jurisdiction: GB; basis: argument
