# Drafting and generation

_Status: anchor; family: legal_task._

## Definition

Generating legal text such as contracts, briefs, opinions, or answers.

## Claims about the concept

### Normative

**geographical_proxy:CN**

- Li et al. (2024) argue that To improve LLM performance at discerning and evaluating legal content and at generating well-formatted legal texts, the structured information within legal documents should be leveraged and more rational training objectives designed. ### Empirical

**general**

- Hartung et al. (2026) report that Legal text generation and summarization played a rather limited role in Legal NLP over the first decade of the 2013-2024 period but picked up speed in the last few years: text generation has grown remarkably since 2022, whereas summarization has grown too, but not to the same extent. [CLM-0023-009]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: dataset_or_experiment; temporal reference: 2013-2024

**geographical_proxy:CN**

- Li et al. (2024) report that Current LLMs do not yet possess the ability to discern and evaluate legal content (the Discrimination level) and are inefficient at producing well-formatted legal texts (the Generation level), a limitation that arises primarily from the highly specialized and structured nature of legal texts. [CLM-0030-019]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:CN; basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Predictive

**GB**

- Briggs of Westbourne (2026) argue that AI is going to revolutionise access to justice for litigants in person: publicly available LLM platforms, free or cheaply subscribed, can generate passable Particulars of Claim from a lay complainant's description of a grievance and give basic legal advice without any lawyer, which is likely to lead to a tsunami of small to moderate value civil claims. [CLM-0047-006]. — jurisdiction: GB [jurisdiction inferred]; basis: argument

**general**

- Nay et al. (2023) argue that Even without replacing trained lawyers, LLMs can assist a lawyer or produce a first draft for the lawyer to check, which could significantly increase lawyers' productivity, decrease the cost of legal services and improve access to legal counsel for people who currently cannot afford it; LLMs could also provide useful legal information to consumers not engaging a traditional lawyer. [CLM-0024-014]. — jurisdiction: general [jurisdiction inferred]; basis: argument

**geographical_proxy:GB**

- Briggs of Westbourne (2026) argue that AI-generated claims may initially contain fakes, mistakes and hallucinations, but the development of AI technology can be expected eventually to iron out those issues. [CLM-0047-007]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:GB [jurisdiction inferred]; basis: case_law (unclear)

### Methodological

**general**

- Li et al. (2024) argue that Rouge-L, used as the main metric for legal text generation tasks, may not be able to fully and accurately present LLMs' performance in the legal domain. [CLM-0030-026]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: argument
