# Multi-Legal-Bench: Evaluating LLMs on Legal Reasoning Across Jurisdictions, Languages, and Legal Traditions

_Ovcharov, Volodymyr (2026), unknown (not printed in the text). Source file: `raw/W0842__Multi-Legal-Bench_Evaluating_LLMs_on_Legal_Reasoning_Across_Jurisdicti.md`._

## What the source argues

Ovcharov (2026) argues that existing legal benchmarks are single-language or non-comparable across jurisdictions [CLM-0036-001], presents Multi-Legal-Bench as the first to run identical tasks on native court decisions from six legal systems [CLM-0036-002], and notes no prior benchmark for Ukrainian, Polish, Czech or Lithuanian law [CLM-0036-003]. It finds few-shot effects task- rather than language-dependent, replicating the UA-Legal-Bench pattern [CLM-0036-004] as a format signal [CLM-0036-005]; no model dominates across jurisdictions [CLM-0036-006], so model selection must be task- and jurisdiction-specific [CLM-0036-007]; cross-lingual transfer does not follow language proximity [CLM-0036-008], tokenizer fertility does not predict accuracy [CLM-0036-009], so tokenizer-centric fixes may have diminishing returns [CLM-0036-010] though fertility orders languages consistently [CLM-0036-011]; the task difficulty ordering is universal [CLM-0036-012], label granularity drives cross-jurisdiction differences [CLM-0036-013], evaluation methodology dominates norm-extraction scores [CLM-0036-014], small models lag most on court-type classification in under-represented languages [CLM-0036-015], and the source defends a sparse task-jurisdiction matrix [CLM-0036-016] and native rather than translated corpora [CLM-0036-017].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Ovcharov (2026) state that Before Multi-Legal-Bench, no benchmark evaluated identical legal tasks on native court decisions from multiple national legal systems using frontier LLMs; Multi-Legal-Bench is the first legal benchmark with identical tasks evaluated across multiple jurisdictions. [CLM-0036-002]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: literature
- Ovcharov (2026) state that No prior benchmark exists for Ukrainian, Polish, Czech, or Lithuanian legal reasoning. [CLM-0036-003]. — jurisdiction: UA, PL, CZ, LT (cumulative); basis: literature

### Normative

[CLM-0036-007]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Empirical

- Ovcharov (2026) report that Few-shot prompting effects on legal classification tasks are task-dependent, not language-dependent: the pattern found on Ukrainian court decisions (judgment-form classification benefits from few-shot examples everywhere, case-outcome prediction shows mixed effects everywhere, court-type classification is at ceiling everywhere) replicates across French, Dutch, Polish, Czech and Lithuanian court decisions. [CLM-0036-004]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Few-shot examples act mainly as a format signal: they help on judgment-form classification, where document headers are distinctive, but inflate prompt length on case-outcome prediction without proportional gain in signal; the format signal is useful even when the examples are written in a language unrelated to the test data. [CLM-0036-005]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:UA (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that No single LLM dominates legal classification tasks across jurisdictions: model rankings shift with both task and jurisdiction, so a model recommended on the basis of English or French legal evaluation may perform poorly on Polish or Czech legal text. [CLM-0036-006]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Cross-lingual few-shot transfer quality on legal classification tasks does not follow language proximity: Ukrainian examples transfer better to French court-type classification (-2.1 pp versus native examples) than to Polish (-13.7 pp) despite the greater linguistic distance, and the data suggest that transfer effectiveness depends more on label-set compatibility between source and target than on language family. The finding rests on only two tasks across five target jurisdictions and needs broader task coverage to confirm its generality. [CLM-0036-008]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that In a cross-lingual setting, tokenizer fertility does not significantly predict zero-shot accuracy on legal classification tasks (r = -0.27, p = 0.14, n = 71, across a 2.3x fertility spread), because model pretraining composition dominates tokenizer efficiency when comparing across languages; fertility predicts inference cost, not quality. [CLM-0036-009]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Across all models, tokenizer fertility on legal text follows the same language ordering (French < Dutch < Czech ≈ Polish < Ukrainian < Lithuanian), an ordering that reflects script, morphological complexity and representation in pretraining corpora. [CLM-0036-011]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that The difficulty ordering of legal tasks for LLMs — court-type classification easiest, then judgment-form classification, then cause-category prediction, with case-outcome prediction hardest — is stable across six civil-law jurisdictions with different languages and label sets, which suggests that the cognitive demands of each task type are inherent to the task structure rather than artifacts of a particular language. [CLM-0036-012]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Label granularity, not language difficulty, is the primary driver of cross-jurisdictional performance differences on legal classification tasks; the spread of court-type classification accuracy from 100% (Czech, one class) to 52% (Polish, six classes) is a label-count effect rather than a language effect, so label granularity confounds cross-jurisdictional comparison. [CLM-0036-013]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Measured legal norm-extraction performance across jurisdictions is dominated by evaluation methodology (exact string matching versus normalised matching of norm references), not by model capability, so that cross-jurisdictional comparison of norm extraction requires jurisdiction-specific normalisation pipelines. [CLM-0036-014]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:UA, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Ovcharov (2026) report that Small and medium LLMs (3-12B parameters) lag frontier models most on court-type classification, with the largest gaps on under-represented languages (Lithuanian -20 pp, Dutch -16 pp), while judgment-form and cause-category classification are more robust to model scale; this suggests that topic classification relies on shallow features small models capture adequately, whereas court-type classification requires deeper document understanding. [CLM-0036-015]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:FR, geographical_proxy:NL, geographical_proxy:PL, geographical_proxy:CZ, geographical_proxy:LT (cumulative); basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Predictive

- Ovcharov (2026) argue that Tokenizer-centric approaches to improving multilingual legal performance, such as vocabulary expansion, may yield diminishing returns compared with including more legal text in each language during pretraining. [CLM-0036-010]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Methodological

- Ovcharov (2026) argue that Existing legal NLP benchmarks either evaluate a single language or aggregate tasks that differ fundamentally from one jurisdiction to another, which makes cross-lingual comparison impossible: when the tasks differ, performance differences confound language ability with task difficulty. [CLM-0036-001]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: literature
- Ovcharov (2026) argue that A cross-jurisdictional legal benchmark should use a deliberately sparse task-jurisdiction matrix that honestly represents real-world metadata availability, neither fabricating labels where registry metadata is absent nor using LLM-generated pseudo-labels; the sparsity is itself informative because it reflects the heterogeneity of open judicial data. [CLM-0036-016]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: argument
- Ovcharov (2026) argue that Cross-lingual legal evaluation should draw from native judicial corpora in each jurisdiction rather than translate a single dataset, because native corpora preserve authentic legal language, jurisdiction-specific terminology and domain reasoning patterns that translation flattens. [CLM-0036-017]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: argument

### Extracted relations (citation-grounded)

- Ovcharov (2026) give reasons for Ovcharov's claim that Few-shot prompting effects on Ukrainian legal tasks are sharply task-dependent and cannot be predicted from task difficulty alone: 3-shot prompting consistently helps judgment form classification (six of seven frontier models gain 8.6-17.7 pp), has mixed effects on case-outcome prediction (macro-F1 rises for most models but falls for some), gives modest consistent gains on cause category prediction (+2.2 to +5.6 pp), has negligible effect on norm extraction, and is minimal on case-type classification because of a ceiling effect. Few-shot prompting can therefore simultaneously help and hurt the same model on different tasks, and legal AI practitioners cannot assume it will improve performance without measuring the effect per task. [CLM-0036-004, CLM-0035-005]. Note: Multi-Legal-Bench names its companion UA-Legal-Bench and reports that the task-dependent few-shot pattern found on Ukrainian decisions reproduces across six jurisdictions, offered as evidence for that finding.
