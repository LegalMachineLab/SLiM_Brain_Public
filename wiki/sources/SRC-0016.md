# LLMs Provide Unstable Answers to Legal Questions

_Blair-Stanek, Andrew, Van Durme, Benjamin (2025), ACM conference proceedings (venue name not printed; ACM template placeholders). Source file: `raw/W0368__v2__LLMs_Provide_Unstable_Answers_to_Legal_Questions.md`._

## What the source argues

Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that gpt-4o, claude-3.5 and gemini-1.5 give unstable answers to hard legal questions even with temperature 0 and every determinism setting fixed [CLM-0016-001], defining stability as agreement across repeated identical calls [CLM-0016-002] and distinguishing it from confidence [CLM-0016-003]; they note that instability has barely been studied and never on legal questions [CLM-0016-004] and that its causes cannot be established for proprietary models [CLM-0016-005]. Their 500 questions are distilled from 2-1 federal appellate decisions [CLM-0016-006] with names changed to defeat memorisation [CLM-0016-007]. Instability is largely model-specific [CLM-0016-008], correlates with length only for gemini [CLM-0016-009], and does not stem from unsound analysis but from different weighing of arguments [CLM-0016-010]; models agree with the court only slightly better than chance, or worse [CLM-0016-011], and agree with each other far more than with the court [CLM-0016-012]; o1 is unstable too [CLM-0016-013]. Since lawyers assume such tools are deterministic [CLM-0016-014], the source counsels caution before real-world legal use [CLM-0016-015] and notes that results on proprietary models cannot be guaranteed reproducible [CLM-0016-016].

## Claims

### Descriptive

- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) state that There has been little research on the instability of large language models, and none involving legal questions. [CLM-0016-004]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: literature
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) state that Why leading LLMs are unstable even with temperature 0 and a fixed seed cannot be determined with certainty, because the models are proprietary; possible causes are nondeterminism in the ordering of floating-point accumulation, cloud hosting in which identical API calls are handled by different servers with slightly different floating-point implementations, and parallelisation across servers or processors whose varying loads change the order of execution. [CLM-0016-005]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: literature

### Normative

[CLM-0016-015]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Empirical

- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that Leading large language models (gpt-4o, claude-3.5 and gemini-1.5) are unstable when answering hard legal questions: even with temperature set to 0 and every other available parameter set for determinism, the identical question sometimes yields the answer that one party should prevail and sometimes the answer that the other party should prevail. Each of the three models is unstable on some share of the 500 questions tested, from 10.6% for claude-3.5 up to 50.4% for gemini-1.5. [CLM-0016-001]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that Most of what makes an LLM unstable on a given legal question is idiosyncratic and specific to that model: there is surprisingly little overlap between the questions that cause instability in gpt-4o, claude-3.5 and gemini-1.5, and the pairwise correlations between the three models' stabilities across the 500 questions, though statistically significant, are small (from 0.123 to 0.181). [CLM-0016-008]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that For gemini-1.5, the longer a legal question is, the less stable the model is on it (a statistically significant negative correlation between stability and question length), whereas for gpt-4o and claude-3.5 the correlation between stability and question length is not statistically significant. [CLM-0016-009]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that LLM instability on legal questions does not arise from unsound legal analysis: when the same model reaches opposite conclusions on the same question in different runs, the legal analysis in each run is sound and the runs simply weigh the arguments or interests differently, as where an ambiguous standard (such as whether a restriction is no more extensive than necessary to serve the state interest) is found met in one run and not met in another. [CLM-0016-010]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that Measured against the party that actually prevailed in the court decision from which each question was distilled, gpt-4o and claude-3.5 perform slightly better than chance with statistical significance, while gemini-1.5 performs worse than chance. [CLM-0016-011]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that The three LLMs agree with each other on which party should prevail far more often than any of them agrees with the actual court decision; possible explanations are that the summarisation of each case into five paragraphs biases the question in one direction, whereas the court had full briefing and the full record, or that similar legal training corpora and training methods make the models tend to answer in the same way. [CLM-0016-012]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) report that OpenAI's o1, whose temperature is hardwired at 1.0 and which is therefore inherently non-deterministic, is unstable on 28% (±12%) of a 50-question sample of the legal questions; this makes o1 more unstable than claude-3.5 but, with 96% probability, more stable than gpt-4o, possibly because o1's deeper reasoning mechanism leads to more consistent outcomes or because its API calls are parallelised differently, hypotheses that cannot be tested given the closed nature of both models. [CLM-0016-013]. — jurisdiction: geographical_proxy:US; basis: dataset_or_experiment

### Conceptual

- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) argue that An LLM is 'stable' if it reaches the same conclusion when asked the identical question multiple times. Stability on a question is measured as the number of calls returning the most-returned answer divided by the total number of calls (ranging between 50% and 100% for binary questions), and a model is unstable on a question if and only if its observed stability on that question is below 100%. [CLM-0016-002]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: argument
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) argue that Stability is distinct from confidence: confidence describes how certain an LLM is that a single answer it gives is correct, whereas stability describes how dispersed an LLM's answers are across multiple calls with identical input. [CLM-0016-003]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: literature

### Methodological

- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) argue that U.S. federal Courts of Appeals decisions in which the three-judge panel split 2-1 typically present a difficult legal question with two well-written opinions that disagree on the proper resolution, and can therefore be distilled into difficult binary legal questions (which of two parties should prevail) for testing LLMs; cases under 10,000 characters tend to hinge on simple procedural matters and cases over 50,000 characters tend to involve issues too complex to summarise, and diversity-jurisdiction cases are excluded because state law diverges across the 50 states whereas federal law is largely uniform. [CLM-0016-006]. — jurisdiction: US; basis: argument
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) argue that When distilling legal questions from published U.S. federal court opinions for LLM testing, all person and entity names (other than states, countries and government agencies) must be changed, because LLMs have almost certainly seen the original opinions during training, U.S. federal court decisions being uncopyrightable and available from several internet sources. [CLM-0016-007]. — jurisdiction: US; basis: argument
- Blair-Stanek and Van Durme (2025) argue that Because the tested LLMs are proprietary and their APIs and underlying models are developed behind closed doors, experimental results obtained on them cannot be guaranteed to be reproducible in the future. [CLM-0016-016]. — jurisdiction: general; basis: argument
