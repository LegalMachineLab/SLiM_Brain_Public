- `2.0` — three substantive changes: the claim is redefined narrowly as what the paper argues — its thesis (exactly one per source) or a hypothesis it aims to demonstrate — so `claim_role` is `thesis | hypothesis`, the `stance` field and broad extraction are removed, and reported/contested positions are never claims; facets and concepts are merged — the Concept node remains and the former facet grids (legal_task, technique_class, normative_concern) replace the v1 anchor list as the concept vocabulary, with the facet rules carried over as mapping rules (≤3 concepts per family, families never imputed); edge candidate sets are uncapped and jurisdiction is not used to filter them. No records had been extracted under 1.x, so the bump forced no re-extraction.
- `2.1` — the thesis/hypothesis distinction is characterisation, not structure: `claim_role` is removed; the definition of a claim (what the paper argues and what makes its contribution original, illustrated as its thesis or a hypothesis it sets out to demonstrate) is unchanged; the exactly-one-thesis rules (in the gate and at lint) are removed, replaced by an at-least-one-claim-per-source rule enforced at extraction and checked at lint (the gate itself, seeing one record at a time, carries no per-source count); coverage and rendering no longer key on roles. No records had been extracted under 2.0, so the bump forced no re-extraction.

- `3.0` (2026-08-29) — anchoring and fidelity reconstructed after the content evaluation of the first ingest found sentence-hugging in ~17% of claims: `quote`/`location`/`quote_translation` become `quotes` (1–3 anchors, each a ≤50-word verbatim quote with its location, each supporting a distinct component of the one proposition); `fidelity` is redefined as the SEMANTIC adherence of the statement to its anchors — textual overlap is irrelevant, so abstraction is no longer penalised; extraction is propositions-first (derive the argued propositions from the full read, then anchor them); originality/priority assertions are explicitly claims, mandatory ethics-section boilerplate explicitly is not. Requires re-extraction of the entire corpus: the 10 sources ingested under 2.2 are pending re-extraction, and the team decides whether the 2.2 graph is archived or discarded.

The meaning and validity of every existing record are unchanged (each convention codifies what existing records already satisfy, or a field they lack by default), so no re-extraction is required beyond the one already pending under 3.0.
- `3.2` (2026-08-30) — the one-sentence statement constraint is removed after the 3.1 re-extraction showed it capping expressiveness (every statement one sentence despite 59 of 62 claims carrying multiple anchors): the one-proposition limit is semantic, never syntactic — a statement takes as many sentences as the proposition needs (typically one to three), the split test is operational (parts a reader could separately accept or reject, or needing different claim types or jurisdictions, split; parts that stand or fall together stay one claim), and anchors map onto the statement's components. Existing one-sentence records remain valid, so no re-extraction is required; sources whose claims compressed multi-component propositions may be re-extracted at the team's discretion.
